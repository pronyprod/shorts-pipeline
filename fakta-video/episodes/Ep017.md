## Sources (every fact)
| fact | source |
|---|---|
| Largest known single flower; rainforest of Sumatra (also Borneo) | Kew POWO / Plants & Fungi blog; Wikipedia "Rafflesia arnoldii" (checked 21 Nov 2026) |
| 1818: Joseph Arnold collected it in Sumatra on the expedition led by Stamford Raffles (Bencoolen) | Wikipedia "Rafflesia arnoldii" (named by Robert Brown 1821, IPNI) |
| No observable leaves, stems or roots, no chlorophyll; parasite on Tetrastigma vines | Wikipedia "Rafflesia arnoldii"; Britannica "Rafflesia" |
| Flower about 1 m across (largest measured 1.14 m, Mongabay) | Kew Plants & Fungi blog; Wikipedia |
| Smells of rotting flesh | Kew; Wikipedia |
| Bud takes many months (about a year); flower lasts a few days (a week at most) | Wikipedia; Discover Magazine |
| 1993: officially a national "puspa langka" (Presidential Decree No. 4, 1993) | Wikipedia "Rafflesia arnoldii", national flower section (single source: only the year is said) |

## Checked and dropped / changed
- Weight: sources disagree (6.8 / 10 / 11 kg): not stated.
- Size record 1.14 m / 120 cm (2019, West Sumatra) varies: only "sekitar satu meter" said.
- Naming dispute (Jack's R. titan 1820 vs Brown 1821): not mentioned.
- Rafflesia is NOT the titan arum (Amorphophallus, largest unbranched inflorescence): the claim is "largest single flower".
- The flower drawing is schematic (5 petals, spots), diameter = the measuring line = about 1 m. Bengkulu pin is approximate.
- "Tebak" pill on screen reads "Selebar apa bunganya?"; spoken line is "selebar apa?".
