## How to say it
- **Energy:** a friend telling you something surprising. Casual, quick, smiling. Don't read like the news.
- **Stress:** lean on the twist words: *mustahil*, *seribu*, *Tetap kalah*, *Tapi tunggu*, *empat kali*, *Satu lagi*, *nggak muat*.
- **Questions:** let your voice go up at the end of the questions in lines 01, 09 ("Merauke?") and 12.
- **Numbers:** say them in full, exactly as written: "lima ribu dua ratus empat puluh". Never say "lima koma dua ribu".
- **US place names:**
  - *Cape Alava*: "keyp a-LA-va"
  - *West Quoddy Head*: "west KWO-di hed"
  - *Washington*: "WO-sying-ten"
  - The Indonesian way of saying these is also fine.
- **Pauses:** no long pauses inside a line. The editor already puts 0.18 s between lines, so stop cleanly at the end of each line.

## Recording on your phone
1. **Room:** pick a small room with soft things in it: a bedroom, or even standing in front of an open wardrobe full of clothes.
   - Avoid an empty room, a bathroom or a tiled kitchen, because they echo.
   - Turn off the fan, the AC and the TV.
   - Close the window.
2. **Phone:** switch on airplane mode so no notifications ring during a take.
3. **App:**
   - **iPhone:** use *Voice Memos*. Go to Settings → Voice Memos → Audio Quality and pick **Lossless**.
   - **Android:** use the built-in *Recorder*, or the free app *ASR Voice Recorder*. In ASR, set the format to **WAV** or **M4A**, 48 kHz (44.1 kHz is fine too).
4. **Mic position:** hold the phone 15–20 cm from your mouth, about a hand-span. Point the bottom of the phone (where the mic is) at your chin, slightly off to the side, so "p" and "b" sounds don't pop.
5. **Level check:** record one test line and listen with earphones.
   - If it crackles when you speak loudly, move the phone further away.
   - If you can hear the room more than your voice, move closer.
6. **One file per line:** record each line as its own file.
   - Do 2–3 takes of a line if you like, and keep the best one.
   - Leave about half a second of silence before and after you speak. The script trims it automatically.
7. **Naming:** name each file with its line number: `01.m4a`, `02.m4a` … `12.m4a`.
   - Anything that starts with the number also works, e.g. `01 hook.m4a`.
   - Formats that work: m4a, wav, mp3, aac, ogg, opus, flac.
8. **Copying to the PC:** use a USB cable or Google Drive. Put all 12 files in this folder (create it if it's missing):
   `C:\Users\<user>\remotion-studio\fakta-video\recordings\ep001v2\`

## How your voice gets swapped in
Tell Claude "swap in my voice for Ep001V2". Claude then runs three commands:
```
npm run voice:v2:own      # cleans each take, joins them, writes the new timing
npm run captions:v2       # Whisper re-times the captions to YOUR voice
npm run render:ep001v2    # renders the video, -14 LUFS, ~26 MB
```
What happens to your recordings (`scripts/make-voice.mts --own`):
- **Rumble:** sounds below 80 Hz are filtered out.
- **Hiss:** light noise reduction.
- **Level:** each line is brought to the same loudness, so quiet and loud takes match.
- **Silence:** the silence at the start and end of each line is trimmed.

Then every beat of the video moves to match your timing, because the animations are tied to the spoken words, not to fixed seconds.

Afterwards Claude reports:
- how many words Whisper matched;
- your pace in words per minute;
- the new length and loudness.

If one line sounds off, re-record just that line, replace the file and run the same three commands.
